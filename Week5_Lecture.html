Personality and Values

How individual differences, fit, situations and values shape behavior in organizations and influence quality-oriented work.

Quality lens: Better person-job and person-organization fit can support reliable behavior, teamwork, ethical choices and continuous improvement.

Primary source: Robbins & Judge, Organizational Behavior, Updated 18th ed., Global Edition, Chapter 4.

SLIDE 2

Learning Outcomes

  • Describe person-job fit and person-organization fit.
  • Define personality and explain how it is measured and determined.
  • Evaluate MBTI and the Big Five Personality Model.
  • Explain Trait Activation Theory.
  • Contrast terminal and instrumental values.
  • Apply Hofstede’s cultural value dimensions to workplace behavior.
SLIDE 3

Linking Personality and Values to the Workplace

Organizations increasingly consider not only whether a person can perform a specific job, but also whether the person can adapt to changing situations and remain committed to the organization.

Person-Job Fit

Matching job requirements with personality characteristics.

Person-Organization Fit

Compatibility between the individual and the organization, especially values and ways of working.

SLIDE 5

What Is Personality?

Text definition: Personality is “the sum of ways in which an individual reacts to and interacts with others.”

Personality is commonly described through measurable traits. The text presents personality as influenced by both heredity and environment, with substantial stability in adulthood.

SLIDE 6

The Myers-Briggs Type Indicator (MBTI)

The MBTI is a personality test that taps four characteristics and classifies people into one of sixteen personality types.

Extraverted (E) vs Introverted (I)
Sensing (S) vs Intuitive (N)
Thinking (T) vs Feeling (F)
Judging (J) vs Perceiving (P)

The text notes mixed evidence regarding validity and reliability. It can support self-awareness and career guidance, but results tend to be unrelated to job performance.

SLIDE 7

The Big Five Personality Model

The Big Five Model proposes that five basic dimensions encompass most significant variation in human personality.

Conscientiousness

Responsible, dependable, persistent and organized.

Emotional Stability

Calm, self-confident and secure.

Extraversion

Sociable, gregarious and assertive.

Openness to Experience

Imagination, sensitivity and curiosity.

Agreeableness

Good-natured, cooperative and trusting.

SLIDE 8

Exhibit 4-3: How Big Five Traits Influence OB Criteria

The key idea is not that one trait is always “best.” Different traits predict different workplace outcomes.

  • Conscientiousness: strong relationship with job performance, job knowledge, OCB and lower unsafe behavior.
  • Emotional stability: supports coping with stress and stable performance.
  • Extraversion: especially relevant to social interaction, influence and leadership contexts.
  • Openness: particularly relevant to learning, creativity and adaptation.
  • Agreeableness: useful in cooperation, teamwork and interpersonal settings.
SLIDE 9

Personality and Situations

Personality does not operate in isolation. Workplace situations can strengthen, weaken or channel the expression of personality traits.

OB principle: Person + Situation → Behavior → Organizational outcomes.
SLIDE 10

Exhibit 4-4: Trait Activation Theory

Text definition: Trait Activation Theory predicts that some situations, events or interventions “activate” a trait more than others.

Examples from the text:

  • Commission-based compensation may make extraversion more relevant because extraverts can be more reward-sensitive.
  • Jobs that encourage creativity may make openness more predictive of desired behavior.
Quality application: Identify which traits are activated by the actual demands of a quality role rather than assuming one universal personality profile.
SLIDE 11

Other Personality Attributes Relevant to OB

Core Self-Evaluations (CSEs)

Bottom-line conclusions individuals have about their capabilities, competence and worth as a person.

Self-Monitoring

The ability to adjust behavior to external, situational factors.

Proactive Personality

A disposition toward taking initiative, identifying opportunities and creating change.

SLIDE 12

Values

Text definition: Values are basic convictions that a specific mode of conduct or end-state of existence is personally or socially preferable to an opposite or converse mode of conduct or end-state of existence.

Values contain both content and intensity. Ranking values by intensity produces an individual’s value system.

SLIDE 13

Why Values Matter in Organizational Behavior

Values lay the foundation for understanding attitudes and motivation and influence perceptions. Employees enter organizations with ideas about what ought and ought not to be.

Quality implication: Values influence decisions about honesty, customer focus, compliance, teamwork, reporting, responsibility and improvement.
SLIDE 14

Terminal versus Instrumental Values

Terminal Values

Desirable end-states of existence - the goals a person would like to achieve during a lifetime.

Instrumental Values

Preferable modes of behavior or means of achieving terminal values.

Examples discussed in the text include freedom, health and meaning in life as terminal values, and autonomy, discipline, kindness and goal orientation as instrumental values.

SLIDE 15

Hofstede’s Framework

Hofstede’s framework examines cultural value differences using five original dimensions:

  • Power distance
  • Individualism versus collectivism
  • Masculinity versus femininity
  • Uncertainty avoidance
  • Long-term versus short-term orientation

The text also notes the later addition of indulgence versus restraint.

SLIDE 16

Applying Personality and Values to Quality-Oriented Behavior

Quality-sensitive work

Accuracy, documentation, reporting, customer interaction, corrective action and improvement.

Managerial question

Which traits, values and situational cues support the behavior the process actually requires?

Use fit as a management lens, not as a stereotype. Selection, role design, feedback, training and organizational culture all shape behavior.
SLIDE 17

In-Class Activity: Fit for a Quality Role

Select one quality-sensitive role such as internal auditor, customer-service representative, quality improvement team member or laboratory analyst.

  1. Define three critical role demands.
  2. Identify relevant Big Five traits or values.
  3. Explain which job situations activate those traits.
  4. Predict the resulting quality behavior.
  5. Identify one management action that strengthens fit without stereotyping.
Deliverable: Role Demand → Trait/Value → Situation → Behavior → Quality Outcome.
SLIDE 18

Key Takeaways

  • Personality helps explain consistent differences in workplace behavior.
  • Person-job and person-organization fit link individual characteristics to work settings.
  • The Big Five has stronger empirical support than MBTI for predicting work behavior.
  • Trait Activation Theory explains why particular situations make certain traits more relevant.
  • Values influence attitudes, perception, choices and behavior.
  • Quality managers should align people, roles, situations and organizational values while avoiding simplistic labeling.
