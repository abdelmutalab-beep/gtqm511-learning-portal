Core concepts: quick reference

Communication

Exchange information to build shared understanding and coordinate action; check whether recipients understood and can respond.

Four functions

Coordinate behavior; motivate; allow expression of concerns; support informed decisions.

Communication process

Sender → encoding → channel → receiver / decoding → feedback. Noise may distort meaning at any step.

Direction of flow

Downward: goals and changes. Upward: concerns and ideas. Horizontal: handoffs and coordination.

Channel richness

Choose written, email, live call or face-to-face communication by complexity, speed of feedback and cues required.

Effective speaking up

Invite quality concerns, acknowledge them, assign follow-up ownership and report responses back.

Communication plan

Specify audience, message, channel, when to communicate, sender or owner and evidence of understanding.

Performance information

State objective, indicator definition and unit, baseline, target, source, reporting period, owner and response rule.

Balanced measures

Outcome (complaint rate), process (checks on time), balancing (damage rate while speeding deliveries).

Performance conversations

Compare trends and targets, investigate data quality, review causes, assign action and recheck results.

Communication process model

1 • Sender

Form the idea; clarify purpose and target audience.

2 • Encoding

Choose understandable language, symbols or data.

3 • Channel

Select an appropriate medium and timely delivery.

4 • Receiver

Decode, clarify what action is required and provide feedback.

Feedback closes the loop. Noise, assumptions and channel mismatch can affect any stage.

Two-minute briefing preparation

Applied case • Rising Customer Complaints

  1. Which signal should the quality lead communicate, and to whom?
  2. What additional workload, handoff and response-time evidence must be checked?
  3. How should the team investigate before asserting the root cause?
  4. What one change should be tested? Name the responsible owner and follow-up date.
  5. Which outcome and balancing indicators will show whether the change worked?

In-class application: prepare the response as a small group and share your plan in two minutes.

Independent study and reflection

Pre-Class Reflection

Describe a workplace incident where unclear information, a poor channel or missing feedback caused a quality problem. Who needed the information, and what should have been communicated differently?

Post-Class Reflection

Choose one real quality indicator. Explain how you would communicate its definition, baseline, trend and target to relevant people, invite feedback and assign a next step.
